How to print an article without ads

Start with the website’s own printable version or your browser’s simplified article view. Check that the text, images, and ending are complete, then choose a printer or save a PDF file. Pressing Ctrl+P on the original page alone does not guarantee that advertisements will disappear.

The goal behind “print article without ads” is usually a readable copy of the story. There are two ways to get there: simplify its layout, or retain more of the website’s appearance while removing unwanted surrounding content. Try the shortest route below and inspect the result before using paper.

These instructions cover desktop browsers. Web pages vary: a text-heavy story is a better candidate for automatic simplification than an interactive report, gallery, or discussion thread.

Try the website’s printable version first

Open the full article, rather than its homepage card or search result. On web pages with delayed content, let the text and useful images finish loading. If the story has several parts, make sure you are looking at the part you need.

Look near the headline, sharing controls, or end of the story for a Print link or print button. Use it if available, then inspect the proposed pages. A publisher’s printable version may retain details that a general article extractor overlooks, such as a recipe’s quantities or a report’s explanatory notes.

If there is no dedicated option, open the browser’s printing controls. In Chrome, use Ctrl+P on Windows or Linux, or Command+P on Mac. Select your printer for paper, or Save as PDF for a digital copy. Google documents the shortcut and destination selection in its Chrome printing guide.

Look beyond the first sheet. If the print preview still contains advertising, a large navigation area, or pages of recommendations, cancel and try a simplified layout. Changing the destination to PDF saves the output; it does not clean the article.

Use your browser’s article tools

Firefox: choose Simplified

Firefox offers a printing format specifically intended to reduce clutter:

  1. Open the article and choose Print from the Firefox menu.
  2. Expand More settings.
  3. Under Format, choose Simplified.
  4. Check the print preview, select your printer, and print.

If you want a file, select Save to PDF under Destination. Mozilla documents both simplified printing and the available printing options.

There is also a Reader View route: when its icon appears in the address bar, enable it and then print. Reader View is useful for a straightforward story, but it is not available for every page.

If Format is missing, Firefox may not have a simplified version of that page. The setting is also absent if you enabled Reader View first. Check which case applies before looking for a missing control. When neither route captures the whole article, return to the original and use the publisher’s version or selective cleanup.

Safari on Mac: show Reader, then check the output

In Safari, open the article, click the Page menu in the Smart Search field, and choose Show Reader. Apple says the option appears only for articles Reader can display. The resulting view reduces navigation and other distractions; see Apple’s Reader instructions.

Next, choose File > Print and inspect the output. For paper, select your printer. To keep a digital copy, open the PDF menu and choose Save as PDF, then enter a name and location. This is Apple’s documented Mac PDF-saving procedure.

Check the actual pages rather than assuming the reading screen guarantees a complete printed document. If Reader omits a chart or supporting section, try the original article’s printable version instead.

Chrome: distinguish reading from printing

Chrome’s Reading mode supports an immersive full-screen view as well as an optional side panel. Google’s current instructions describe opening it from the page’s right-click menu and adjusting the reading experience.

The Reading mode instructions do not establish a Firefox-style simplified printing workflow. Do not assume that a cleaner reading screen means Ctrl+P will produce the same cleaned article. Inspect what the printing controls actually show. If the original clutter remains, try the site’s printable version or the article-PDF route below.

Save a cleaner article PDF in Chrome

The Website approach can remove identifiable external side columns and content after the detected article ending. An ad embedded inside retained article content can still survive. Keeping source styling is not a promise of exact reproduction or universal ad removal.

For a copy you can inspect before saving:

  1. Open the complete article in Chrome and open the extension.
  2. In the popup’s settings, select Preview PDF. This matters if your saved preference is Quick save or Ask every time.
  3. Open the gear beside Article. Under Style, choose Website or Simple.
  4. If you choose Simple, adjust the available paper, font, page, and orientation controls. For ordinary US printer paper, choose Letter and Multi-page.
  5. Click Article and wait for the generated document to appear.
  6. Check its contents, then click Download to save it or Print to open Chrome’s print dialog.

Website hides the paper, font, page, and orientation controls; switch to Simple if you need those choices. One Page is useful for continuous digital reading, but a long sheet is not automatically a suitable layout for physical paper. Check the size and page breaks when printing it.

The viewer’s Download command saves the document already on screen. You do not need to select Save as PDF again to obtain that copy. Open the downloaded PDF file if you want to keep it, share it, or print it later.

Check the article before printing

A clean-looking first page can hide an incomplete result. Compare these points with the original:

  • Identity: the correct headline, author, and publication date where relevant.
  • Beginning and ending: the first substantive paragraph and the final paragraph, including any conclusion.
  • Useful visuals: charts, photographs, captions, tables, and labels needed to understand the story.
  • Supporting material: footnotes, references, appendices, and links you intend to follow later.
  • Unwanted extras: remaining advertisements, related stories, and the comments section.
  • Paper layout: readable type, unclipped edges, sensible breaks, browser-added headers and footers, and the expected number of sheets.

Article extraction can mistake where a story ends. Material beyond the detected boundary may be excluded even when it matters to you. If a reference or appendix is missing, use a fuller capture rather than accepting the shorter result.

Review headers and footers too. A source address and date can be useful for a research copy, while repeated browser-added lines may be unnecessary for a handout. Keep the information you need to identify the source.

Fix common problems

An advertisement is still inside the story

It may belong to the same retained content block as the article. Try Simple or the browser’s simplified format, then compare the text again. If the result loses useful content, return to the fuller version and hide the unwanted block selectively where your tool supports that action.

Turning off background graphics is not a general ad-removal method. Ads can be ordinary text, images, or embedded content. Use content cleanup for those; treat background graphics as an appearance setting.

A picture or table disappeared

First check that it loaded on the original page. If a diagram uses a background image, check whether background graphics are enabled. Then try the publisher’s printable version or Website style rather than the simplified layout. Confirm that the caption and surrounding explanation remain together. If a figure conveys essential information, preserving it is more important than achieving the shortest document.

The type is tiny or there are too many sheets

Check paper size and scaling. A continuous sheet may shrink substantially when fitted onto paper; a paginated export is usually easier to assess. In Simple, use Multi-page and the intended paper size. For a wide table, try landscape and inspect the edges before printing.

Remove unrelated trailing material before reducing the font. Also check whether headers and footers are taking space you need. Do not solve an oversized document by making the article too small to read.

Only the opening paragraphs appear

Confirm that the complete story is available in the original tab. Login prompts, access restrictions, and unfinished loading can leave a partial article. Sign in through the website if you have access, let the content load, and retry. None of these methods supplies text that the page has not made available to you.

Frequently asked questions

Print article without ads: which no-install options work?

Start with the site’s print button, Firefox’s Simplified format, or Safari’s Reader. Browser extensions become an option when the built-in route does not give you the content or layout you need. Always check the result.

Does disabling background graphics remove advertisements?

Not reliably. It changes whether background graphics appear in the output, but advertising may use other page elements. It can also affect useful visual information. Compare the result with the original, especially for charts or colored labels.

Can I save a copy instead of using paper?

Yes. In Chrome choose Save as PDF; in Firefox choose Save to PDF; on Mac use the PDF menu’s Save as PDF command. In the extension’s finished viewer, choose Download. Saving first gives you a document to inspect and print later.

Why did the article’s font and layout change?

Simplified views rebuild web pages into a reading layout. That can improve readability while changing typography and spacing. Use Website style or the publisher’s printable version when the original presentation matters, and check that important content survives either choice.
